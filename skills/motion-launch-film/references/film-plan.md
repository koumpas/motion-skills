# Compact film plan

Use this for a new film with several scenes. Scale the detail to the request.
The plan should explain decisions that affect the final picture.

## Brief

Record viewer, product action, visible result, intended response, duration, format, and audio.
Separate supplied facts from assumptions and illustrative content.

## Concept

Record the selected concept and the reason for it.
If alternatives matter, name the rejected concepts and their practical limits.
Select a visual system from the brand: typography, color meaning, material, light, and camera behavior.

## Beat table

| Time | What the viewer understands | Visible event and source | Main subject | Text and reading time | Motion | Transition reason |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Start and end | One concrete point | Action, result, and captured asset | One main subject | Exact copy and usable time | Start/end state, duration, curve | Why the next shot follows |

Time values are a plan. Revise them when the content or reading time requires it.
Do not use a rigid shot count or a universal end-card duration.
For each important transition, record the last useful state and the next useful state.
Plan a short inspection around that boundary.

## Worked example: a hypothetical crop tool

Brief: a silent 10-second film that shows one image become two useful social crops.
The feature and screens are illustrative. The example makes no claim about fal product behavior.

Concept A: show the crop task and its results.
Concept B: show a sequence of changing aspect-ratio frames.
Concept C: show a kinetic phrase about composition.

Select A because the viewer can see the feature's consequence with little explanation.
Use the supplied image as the visual source across both crops.
Keep its subject stable while the frame changes.

| Time | Understanding | Visible event | Main subject | Text | Motion | Transition reason |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0–2 s | One image is the source | Show image and a crop selection | Image subject | One short phrase if needed | Selection enters and settles | Selection starts the crop task |
| 2–6 s | The frame changes; the subject stays useful | Resolve into two crop results | Active crop, then second crop | Label only if needed | Frame reshapes; camera reframes | First result leads to the second format |
| 6–8 s | Both results come from the same image | Reveal the two outputs | Outputs as one comparison | Optional result statement | Pull back to show both results | Completion makes the result readable |
| 8–10 s | The viewer knows the next action | Show supplied brand mark and CTA | CTA | Exact supplied CTA | Settle into a readable finish | The task is complete |

Test the crop transition first. Check image bounds, subject position, and readable output labels.
For a later copy edit, retain the image, crop geometry, transition timing, and camera motion unless the new copy requires a change.
